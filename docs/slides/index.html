For whom, and why

Volleyball
Statistics Tracker

For fans and amateur players who want to see how an individual played across matches.

0:00–0:50. Start with a question: if you know VakifBank won both matches, what do you know about how Marina Markova attacked? You still need to find her row and work out how many attempts scored.

Official CEV reports contain the detail, but the tables are dense and read one match at a time. I wanted a fan or amateur player to be able to open a match, find a player and put two appearances side by side. I’ll use two matches between the same clubs to show exactly what that looks like.

The demo

Marina Markova across two matches

8 Jan 2026VakifBank won 3–1 · Markova scored 21

4 Feb 2026VakifBank won 3–2 · Markova scored 32

0:50–2:15. I load the January and February CSVs. Each opens a match report with the set scores and team totals. Under Players, I choose Markova and see 21 points in the first match and 32 in the second. Both reports stay in the browser’s saved list and can be reopened.

Point at the player selector in the screenshot. With Markova selected, the comparison shows both saved appearances together. Read bottom to top for match chronology. Her attack success goes from 43.2% in January to 55.6% in February. That means a larger share of her attacks scored in February. Attack efficiency also rises from 22.7% to 35.2%, and reception error rate drops from 11.4% to 2.6%. These are two matches against the same opponent.

Data: CEV reports 82223 and 82229. Screenshot: a real run of the browser CSV demo.

Why it works

From CEV match 82295, I wrote down Gabi’s 14 attack points, 26 attempts and 17 total points.

Changing the attack-points column from cells[21] to cells[22] made the Gabi test fail.

Restored mapping

With cells[21] restored, the test passed and the tracker showed all three expected values.

Regression test · Recorded check

2:15–3:25. I picked one field that matters to the demo: a player’s attack points. Before running the program I checked the official report and wrote down the three expected values shown here. The agent helped put those values into a regression test against the saved report.

Then we deliberately moved the parser one column to the right. Ask what should happen. Gabi’s test went red, as intended, because the parser could no longer read her attack points. Restoring the column made it green again. I also checked the displayed results against my original numbers. The expected values came from the report I checked before the test ran.

What I learned

A mismatch can be rounding

In match 82294, 22 of 24 team totals and rates matched CEV exactly. Both differences were attack success rates.

73 ÷ 139 ≈ 52.5%

The tracker shows 52.5%. CEV rounds the same rate to 53%.

Repository moment: the match 82294 comparison

3:25–4:25. In the repository spike, I asked whether the team totals calculated by the tracker matched CEV cell by cell. The diff showed two different percentages even though all the underlying counts agreed. For this team, we can check the shared counts: 73 attacks scored out of 139 attempts. The resulting rate rounds to 52.5% at one decimal and 53% at a whole percentage.

The concrete lesson is to trace a displayed rate back to its numerator and denominator before correcting a parser. The repository keeps the source HTML, the comparison script and the diff, so this particular result can be reproduced.

What’s next

Match dates in the comparison

Show when each match was played and order the comparison by match date when both dates are known.

I’d load February first, then January. The comparison should still read January, then February.

4:25–5:00. The comparison currently uses the time the report was saved. That was enough to reopen a report, but makes the story depend on upload order. I’d capture the match date where it’s available and keep the save time available in the saved-match list. For an older CSV with no match date, I’d show that the date is unknown. My first check would be to load these same two reports in reverse order and confirm that the comparison still reads in match order. End on the link.
